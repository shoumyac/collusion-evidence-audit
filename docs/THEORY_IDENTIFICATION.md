# What can an audit of an instructed LLM agent identify? Theory and checks

Status: draft for paper v5. Code: `analysis/theory_checks.py`, output `analysis/theory_checks_results.json`. All numbers below come from that file (our three open-weight models, 10,730 runs).

## 0. Why this is needed

Our report finds that the "collude" instruction raises prices even when the rival's messages are swapped for another run's, and concludes "parallel obedience, not communication". Reading the code of the swap control (`games/runner.py`) while preparing this theory showed that the conclusion is stronger than the design supports:

* the swap replaces only the rival's message text; the rival's chosen action stays visible in the prompt history;
* the replayed text comes from another run of the same condition, so its marginal law equals the live one.

Section 3 shows that a mean contrast under such a replay is exactly zero for one-round responsiveness of any strength, and measures only a feedback component over several rounds. So the swap null is a sanity check, not a test of message mediation. The paper's text should say so (Section 7). The theory below fixes what each control identifies, and gives the control that does identify the message channel.

## 1. Setup

Two agents (A, B). Instruction z in {0 (honest), 1 (collude)}. Each round an agent emits a message m and an action a, simultaneously, then both see the other's message and action. Let h be the history. Write B's action as

    a_B,t = f(z, m_A,<t, a_A,<t, h_B,<t, U)

with U exogenous sampling noise (temperature). Define potential outcomes Y(z, m) = f(z, m, ., U): B's action under instruction z when shown rival text m, other history fixed. Let M_z be the live law of the rival's text under instruction z.

Estimands:

* Total effect TE = E Y(1, M_1) - E Y(0, M_0), what the report calls "the effect of the instruction".
* Direct (instruction-only) effect DE = E Y(1, M_0) - E Y(0, M_0): the instruction acting through B's own prompt, with B reading honest-condition text.
* Text-mediated effect TM = E Y(1, M_1) - E Y(1, M_0): what changes when a colluding B reads colluding text rather than honest text.

TE = DE + TM exactly. "Parallel obedience" is the claim TM = 0. "Communication-mediated" is TM > 0.

## 2. Interfaces and the separation result

Audit interfaces, from weakest to strongest:

* L0: marginal law of the action (price level). 
* L1: the live joint law of (z, messages, actions).
* L2: same-condition replay: B reads text drawn from M_z independently of its own run.
* L3: cross-condition replay: B with instruction z reads text drawn from M_(1-z).

**Theorem 1 (L1 cannot identify responsiveness).** For any live law P of (m_A, a_B) there is a responsive model (B reads m_A) and a common-cause model (B reads nothing from A; both draw from a shared seed) that both generate P. They differ under replay iff f depends on its text argument.

*Proof.* By noise outsourcing (Kallenberg, Lemma 3.22), for any joint law of (X, Y) on Borel spaces there is f and U uniform, independent of X, with Y = f(X, U) in law. Responsive model: B plays f(m_A, U). Common-cause model: draw s = (m_A, U) once, A emits m_A, B plays f(s_1, s_2) without reading the channel. Both have joint law P. Replace A's text by an independent copy m': the first plays f(m', U), the second still plays f(m_A, U). These have different laws iff f varies in its first argument on a set of positive probability. QED.

Numerical witness (Gaussian, `A_witness`): common seed s, m_A = s + e, B plays s + e' (common cause) or rho * m_A + u with rho = Var(s)/Var(m_A) (responsive). Live Cov(m_A, a_B) is 1.001 and 1.001, Var(a_B) is 1.997 and 2.003, i.e. the same joint law. After replay the covariance of B's action with the replayed message is -0.002 versus 0.998.

**Corollary (L0 below L1).** Xu et al. build, for any given marginal law, joint laws with and without coordination; a test of the marginal alone has power equal to its size against such alternatives. Our price-level detectors are L0.

## 3. What same-condition replay (L2) identifies

Let the replayed text m' be drawn from M_z independently of B's own run, and let the live law be generated by a sender whose text may depend on B's past. Define the swap contrast S = E[a_B under replay] - E[a_B live].

**Theorem 2.** (a) One round, or any design where A's text is independent of B's history and noise: S = 0 for every f, however responsive.
(b) Over several rounds, S = integral of f d(P_(m') x P_(h,U) - P_(m,h,U)): it is non-zero only through dependence between the sender's text and B's own earlier path, i.e. reciprocal feedback.

*Proof.* (a) m' has the same marginal as m and is independent of (h, U); so is m under the independence assumption. Hence the two integrals coincide. (b) is the displayed identity. QED.

Numerically (`A_witness`): mean contrast replay minus live is 0.000 for the common-cause model and 0.000 for the responsive model with rho = 0.5.

So the swap null in the report supports "no reciprocal-feedback component in the mean". It does not support TM = 0, because replayed same-condition text carries the same collusive content as the live text. The paper's price-flag result (100% of swapped collusion runs flagged) has the same reading.

## 4. What identifies the text channel (L3), and a usable estimator on our data

**Theorem 3 (cross-condition replay).** If text is assigned independently of B's run, then TM is identified by the contrast of colluding B reading text from M_1 versus text from M_0, up to the visible-action channel (the replay holds the rival's visible action fixed, so what is identified is the effect of text conditional on the action it accompanies). Proof: randomisation of the text argument, as in a factorial experiment.

In the Gaussian witness with a mean shift of 1.5 between conditions: contrast 0 (common cause) versus 0.75 = rho * 1.5 (responsive).

**An estimator available now (`B_replay_regression`).** In swap runs the replayed text is exogenous. We regress each agent's action in round t on a text feature of the replayed rival message of round t-1 (coordination language: the regex of `analysis/say_do.py`), controlling for the rival's visible previous action and the agent's own previous action, with cell by round fixed effects, cluster bootstrap over runs. A placebo uses the rival's own-run text, which the agent never saw. A live regression, confounded by common causes, is reported for contrast.

| model, game, instruction | replay slope [95% CI] | placebo | live |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B pricing collude | 0.00 [-0.01, 0.02] | 0.01 | -0.01 |
| Qwen3-32B pricing collude | 0.01 [-0.01, 0.02] | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| Phi-4 pricing collude | -0.01 [-0.03, 0.01] | 0.00 | -0.01 |
| Phi-4 pricing honest | 0.06 [0.02, 0.11] | 0.06 | 0.06 |

Reading: on prices of 1 to 10, no model's price responds to coordination language in the replayed rival text by more than about 0.03 (collude) or 0.11 (the one honest-condition effect, Phi-4, also present in the placebo). Auction and Cournot slopes have wide intervals (tens of times the pricing widths) and are not informative. Limits: one crude feature; a null on it is not a null on text; slopes condition on the visible rival action.

## 5. Design numbers for the cross-condition replay we propose

With run-mean price as the unit (`E_design`, pricing): effect 1.9 to 2.7 (Qwen3-8B, Phi-4) and 0.6 (Qwen3-32B); run-level SD 0.4 to 0.8. At 50 runs per arm the minimum detectable effect (80% power, two-sided 5%) is 0.22 to 0.45 on the price scale, i.e. a text-mediated share of roughly 10 to 25% of the 8B and Phi-4 effect, and 35 to 65% of the 32B effect. For a share of 50% of the effect, 3 to 8 runs per arm suffice for 8B and Phi-4 (27 to 79 for 32B).

## 5c. Three further results (finite-sample and design)

**Proposition 6 (exact randomisation test for the text channel).** Assign each run's text source by a random offset independent of the run's noise. Under the sharp null that no run's outcome depends on which text it reads, every permutation of source labels over runs is equally likely, so the randomisation p-value of the run-mean contrast has exact size alpha (up to Monte Carlo error), for any within-run dependence. Check (`analysis/theory2.py`, 1,500 simulations, 20 rounds, intraclass correlation 0.5): size 0.051 (20 runs per arm) and 0.055 (10 per arm), against 0.55 for a round-level t-test and 0.055 for a run-level t-test; power at effect 0.6 is 0.714 (randomisation), 0.719 (run-level t) and 0.979 for the round-level test whose size is invalid. Using runs as units costs no power.

**Proposition 7 (rounds cannot buy power).** The run mean has variance sigma_b^2 + sigma_w^2 / m. The minimum detectable effect at n runs per arm is therefore bounded below by 2.8 sigma_b sqrt(2/n) however many rounds m are played. In our pricing cells the between-run variance dominates (Qwen3-8B sigma_b^2 = 0.18 to 0.54, sigma_w^2 = 0.03 to 0.09; Phi-4 0.25 to 0.37 versus 0.02 to 0.07): at 50 runs per arm the detectable effect is 0.38 with 1 round and 0.36 with unbounded rounds (wording 1). Runs, not rounds, are the sample size, which is the design counterpart of Proposition 4.

**Proposition 8 (a same-condition replay extrapolates only if the text feature is sufficient).** Write g(m) = E Y(1, m). Same-condition replay identifies g on the support of M_1. Then TM = integral of g d(M_1 - M_0) is identified from it only if g depends on m through the measured feature s(m) (and M_0 has overlapping support). If g is L-Lipschitz in a metric on texts, then |TM| <= L W_1(M_1, M_0) (Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality). The assumption is testable: compare the extrapolated TM with the cross-condition TM.

Test (`analysis/theory3.py`, Qwen3-8B pricing, feature = coordination language, whose rate is 0.87 to 0.90 in collude text and 0.29 to 0.35 in honest text; the long-run multiplier 1/(1 - rho) uses the own-lag coefficient 0.67 to 0.95): predicted TM is -0.06 [-0.17, -0.01], +0.06 [-0.03, +0.16], -0.07 [-0.21, +0.04] in the three wordings against observed +0.12, +0.02, +0.28 [+0.12, +0.45]. In wording 3 the interval of the prediction excludes the observed value, so the feature is not sufficient there: some text effect runs through content our regex does not measure. The correct reading of Section 4's null is therefore "no effect through coordination language", not "no text effect".

## 6. Unit of replication and the transport of thresholds

**Proposition 4 (size inflation).** If each run contributes m exchangeable-within-run observations with intraclass correlation rho, a two-sample test that treats observations as independent has, under the null and for large samples, size 2(1 - Phi(z_(alpha/2) / sqrt(D))) with D = 1 + (m-1) rho. For D = 11 this is 0.55.

*Proof.* The variance of a group mean is inflated by D relative to the iid formula, so the z-statistic has variance D. QED.

Check (`D_size_inflation`, 25 non-degenerate honest cells, honest runs randomly split in half): predicted and observed round-level size differ by 0.019 on average (max 0.071); for pricing, D = 6.7 to 16.1 and observed size 0.44 to 0.68 against 0.45 to 0.63 predicted, versus 0.04 to 0.07 when runs are the unit. (One degenerate Qwen3-32B cell with identical runs is excluded; it gives 1.00 versus 0.00.)

**Proposition 5 (price thresholds do not transport).** If honest-run prices in family k are location-scale with law F_k, a threshold q fixed at the (1 - alpha) honest quantile of one family has false-positive rate 1 - F_k(q) in family k. It equals alpha for at most one k when the F_k differ, and a threshold that is valid for all k is the maximum of the family quantiles, whose power in family k is 1 - G_k(q) for the collusion law G_k.

Check (`C_auroc`, pricing, honest 95th percentile): pooled AUROC 0.89 while within-model AUROC is 0.72 to 1.00 across 9 model-wording cells; the Qwen3-32B threshold (5.96) gives false-positive rate 0.43 on Qwen3-8B, and the 8B threshold (7.44), valid for all three, detects 0.97 of 8B collusion runs, 0.02 of Qwen3-32B and 0.35 of Phi-4.

## 7. What this changes in the paper

1. Replace "parallel obedience rather than communication" by: the same-condition swap is a feedback test that cannot detect message mediation (Theorem 2); the replay regression finds no text effect on the coordination-language feature in pricing (Section 4), and the cross-condition replay of Section 5 would settle the question.
2. Add the unit-of-replication formula and the threshold-transport result as the formal counterpart to the empirical sections.
3. Add the interface hierarchy L0 to L3 as the organising frame of the evidence ladder.

## 8. Not claimed

* The theorems are elementary (noise outsourcing, randomisation, variance inflation). The contribution is their application to instructed LLM agents and the finding that a widely used control is formally blind to the effect it is read as excluding.
* Section 4 is one feature and three models. The cross-condition replay has been run on one model and one game (Section 5b).
* The decomposition TE = DE + TM depends on the order of conditioning; the other order (text held at M_1) gives a different split when there is interaction between instruction and text.
